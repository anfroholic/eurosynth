EuroSynth Rev 2 — analog voice preview lab

Audio sketches of the analog macros proposed for the second chip (see docs/REV2_ANALOG.md). Unlike the rev-1 playground, nothing here is a bit-exact silicon model — each card is a behavioral circuit simulation in floating point: exponential V/oct converters, tanh differential pairs, ladder & OTA-C filter models, pitch drift, control feedthrough, BBD clock grit. Real silicon will differ (that's the charm) — these previews are for choosing which voices are worth the die area. Rendered live in Python at 24 kHz; every card has ▶ Play and ⬇ WAV. Badge colors = report tiers: A backbone · B character · C research.

⏳ Loading Python (Pyodide)… first load pulls the runtime from a CDN (a few seconds). The voices below activate when it's ready.

A1 Expo VCO — CEM3340 homage

Matched-NPN exponential converter feeding a triangle core: the pitch slider is a literal CV in volts, one octave per volt. Drift adds the analog truth — a slow random walk of a dozen cents.

A2 OTA-C state-variable filter

Two gm-C integrators (bias current = cutoff CV). Crank resonance toward self-oscillation; feed it pings with high reso and it rings like a struck bell — the LPG trick.

B1·B2 Ladder filter — acid line

A 16-step 303-style pattern (slides, accents, rests) through a 4-pole tanh ladder. Transistor = fat Moog; diode = squelchy 303 (resonance loses its lows). Renders take a few seconds.

B3 Wavefolder — West-Coast timbre

A triangle folded by cascaded differential pairs: drive pushes the wave back on itself, harmonics bloom. Symmetry skews the folds (even harmonics — the mismatch knob).

A3 VCA / ring mod — Gilbert cell

One translinear core, three uses: ring (bells & metal), tremolo, and hard VCA chop. Bleed = carrier/CV feedthrough of an imbalanced cell — the honest analog artifact.

A4 Noise — thermal & avalanche

White = amplified resistor hiss. Pink = −3 dB/oct filter bank. Avalanche = the popcorn/burst noise of a junction pushed to breakdown — the 10 V-domain experiment. Wind runs it through a swept bandpass.

B4 Drum voice — bridged-T (808)

Triggered resonators: kick & tom are swept damped sines into a soft-clipping stage, snare adds gated noise, hat is six squares through a highpass. ▶ Groove plays the whole kit.

C1 BBD delay — bucket brigade

A plucked arp through a simulated 1024-stage bucket-brigade line: delay time sets the clock, and short times = bright, long times = dark, exactly like the real chips. Grit adds clock noise, droop and companding crunch.

C2 Chua's circuit — analog chaos

The canonical chaotic circuit (inductor replaced by a gyrator, as it would be on-die), integrated live. Low α = a plain limit-cycle tone; nudge it up and it period-doubles into the double-scroll growl.

C3 Lunetta bank — CMOS drone

A bank of Schmitt-trigger relaxation oscillators (standard cells misused, CD40106-style). Near-unison spread = thick beating drone; XOR the outputs through logic gates for ring-mod shimmer.

★ The full Rev-2 voice — VCO → folder → ladder → VCA

The whole proposed signal chain playing a sequence: expo VCO saw, envelope-driven wavefolder, transistor ladder with envelope-swept cutoff, VCA. Fold drive at 0 = pure East-Coast subtractive; push it for the West-Coast bite. (Longest render on the page — the entire chip per sample.)

What you're hearing. Behavioral analog previews, not golden vectors: each card implements the circuit idea from docs/REV2_ANALOG.md — exponential converters, tanh differential-pair saturation, one-pole-per-stage ladder models, a trapezoidal-integration OTA-C filter, random-walk pitch drift, Gilbert-cell feedthrough, BBD clock bandwidth — in floating-point Python at 24 kHz. The real macros will be designed in xschem, verified in ngspice, and laid out by hand; fabricated silicon will drift, mismatch and clip in its own ways. Use this page to decide which voices earn a place on the die, not as a promise of the exact sound.
